Abstract
Post-training quantization enables efﬁcient deployment of large language models,1
but its effects on multilingual safety, particularly in low-resource languages, re-2
main unclear. We conduct a pre-registered audit of 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4) quan-3
tization in Qwen3-4B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct across 10 languages, evaluating4
multilingual capability and safety refusal. NF4 substantially degrades Llama-3.1-5
8B capability, with losses increasing from −9.4 percentage points in English to6
−13.7 points in low-resource languages, supporting our capability-degradation7
hypothesis. In contrast, we ﬁnd no evidence of a low-resource-speciﬁc safety-8
refusal effect ( β = 0 .027, p = 0 .88), and capability loss does not correlate with9
refusal changes ( r = 0 .010, p = 0 .97). An apparent −58.9-point refusal col-10
lapse in Llama low-resource languages reproduces under a veriﬁed NF4 run, but11
analysis of 945 outputs indicates severe generation degeneration and automated12
scoring artifacts rather than actionable harmful assistance. Blinded human valida-13
tion (n = 200) and tri-stream sensitivity analysis further support the robustness of14
these ﬁndings.15
1 Introduction16
Deploying large language models (LLMs) in resource-constrained environments often requires post-17
training weight quantization [ Dettmers et al. , 2023]. Low-resource languages face limited native-18
language safety coverage [ Y ong et al., 2023, Li et al. , 2024], while the same languages can experi-19
ence larger capability losses under quantization. We test whether 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4) quanti-20
zation accelerates this disparity in underrepresented languages (evaluated on Bengali, Swahili, and21
Javanese). We evaluate benchmark-level safety refusal and reading comprehension; our data estab-22
lish a benchmark-level empirical phenomenon rather than population-level social harms.23

2 Related Work41
Quantization in Large Language Models. Weight quantization reduces memory bandwidth bot-42
tlenecks by discretizing ﬂoating-point tensors into low-bit representations [ Lin et al. , 2023, Frantar43
et al. , 2022]. NF4 [ Dettmers et al. , 2023] constructs an optimal quantile distribution for normally44
distributed weights. Existing evaluations focus almost exclusively on English benchmarks, leaving45
non-English alignment unexplored.46
Cross-Lingual Alignment and Evaluator Fragility. Multilingual jailbreak studies show that47
frontier LLMs often fulﬁll harmful requests translated into low-resource languages [ Y ong et al.,48
2023, Deng et al. , 2023], driven by uneven cross-lingual representations [ Li et al. , 2024]. Re-49
cent work shows that LLM safety evaluators are vulnerable to superﬁcial input artifacts [ Chen &50
Goldfarb-Tarrant, 2025] and exhibit poor reliability in low-resource languages [ Fu & Liu , 2025],51
motivating our investigation of evaluator–degeneration interactions.52
Mechanistic Representations of Safety. Refusal in aligned models often operates via low-53
dimensional directional subspaces (Geva et al., 2023), suggesting a hypothesis for why safety refusal54
and comprehension might show differential sensitivity to weight compression.55
3 Methods and Experimental Architecture56
Models and Inference. We evaluate Qwen3-4B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct in native BF16 and57
4-bit NF4 via BitsAndBytes using greedy decoding ( temp=0, seed=42, max_tokens=2048). Con-58
ﬁrmatory Llama-3.1-8B-NF4 inference uses a fresh provenance-veriﬁed reproduction arm on an59
NVIDIA H100 GPU ( meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct revision 0e9e39f, BitsAndBytes60
0.50.2, PyTorch 2.14, Transformers 5.16.1; llama_nf4_v2), retaining historical runs as replication61
evidence.62
Tasks, Datasets, and Stratiﬁcation. The safety task uses MultiJAIL [ Deng et al. , 2023] (31563
parallel adversarial prompts × 10 languages = 3,150 items per model variant). The control task64
uses Belebele [ Bandarkar et al. , 2023] (900 parallel 4-choice items × 10 languages = 9,000 items65
per model variant). Languages span four tiers [ Joshi et al. , 2020]: Reference (en), HRL (zh, it,66
vi), MRL (ar, ko, th), and LRL (bn, sw, jv).67
Tri-Stream Refusal Adjudication. Automated classiﬁers like WildGuard [ Han et al. , 2024]68
can conﬂate non-refusal with format degeneration. We establish targeted blinded human vali-69
dation ( n=200 frozen items in human_review_packet_blinded_FINAL.csv) alongside three70
blinded AI judges (Terra GPT-5.6, Google Gemini, Qwen-14B). Human adjudications serve vali-71
dation/governance rather than gold-standard ground truth. We evaluate: Stream A (automated base-72
line); Stream B (human-primary adjudication with Jeffreys-smoothed cell priors); and Stream C73
(worst-case boundary reclassifying all A MBIGUOUS responses as N ON_R EFUSAL ).74
Llama NF4 Governance Gate. Automated scoring of Llama-3.1-8B-NF4 in LRL indicated an75
apparent −58.9 pp drop. Root-cause audit revealed degenerate token repetition loops scored as76
non-refusal despite containing zero harmful compliance. This stratum was quarantined behind a77
Governance Gate (PRE_A DJUDICATION _D ESCRIPTIVE ) and excluded from conﬁrmatory GLMM78
inference.79

4.1 Inter-Rater Reliability and Adjudication Validation84
Table 1 summarizes inter-rater agreement on the frozen N =200 targeted blinded human valida-85
tion dataset (human_review_packet_blinded_FINAL.csv). Across the 3-model evaluation panel86
(N =85), Majority AI consensus achieves substantial agreement with human labels ( κ = 0 .74,87
84.7% agreement). Across the full N =200 frozen dataset, Qwen-14B achieves 69.5% overall agree-88
ment ( κ = 0 .45), reﬂecting high refusal recall ( 95.0%, 95/100) alongside automated over-refusal89
on degraded non-refusals (39/92). Pairwise agreement among individual AI judges across the 836-90
item pool spans κ = 0 .32–0.48, indicating varied decision boundaries across models rather than a91
uniform artifact. Disagreements concentrate along the subtle R EFUSAL vs. A MBIGUOUS boundary.92
4.2 Capability–Safety Dissociation93
H2 (Capability Degradation) is Supported for Llama-3.1-8B. Primary empirical outcomes (Ta-94
ble 2, Figure 1) using the veriﬁed llama_nf4_v2 arm show that capability degradation on Bele-95
bele scales monotonically with linguistic distance from English for Llama-3.1-8B: −9.4 pp (REF)96
→ − 9.6 pp (HRL) → − 11.6 pp (MRL) → − 13.7 pp (LRL), supporting H2. Qwen3-4B displays97
non-English loss (−6.8 to −8.8 pp across HRL–LRL) without a strict monotonic tier gradient.98
H1 (Safety Degradation) is Not Supported. Across non-gated strata, refusal changes do not show99
consistent NF4-induced degradation. In Qwen3-4B, LRL refusal increases from 25.0% to 33.3%100
(+8.3 pp), English drops marginally ( −1.9 pp), and HRL decreases by −3.8 pp. For non-gated101
Llama-3.1-8B, refusal shifts remain minimal ( −1.4 to +0.9 pp). Matched-pair analysis ( N =2,489)102
conﬁrms symmetry: 185 instances (7.4%) transitioned Refusal → Non-Refusal, while 166 (6.7%)103
transitioned Non-Refusal → Refusal (McNemar p = 0.33).104
Language-Level Capability vs. Safety Association. At the model–language arm level, the as-105
sociation between ∆Capability and ∆Safety is near zero ( r = 0 .010 across 17 non-gated arms,106
p = 0 .97; Figure 2, Appendix A): Qwen3-4B r = 0 .151 (p = 0 .68); non-gated Llama-3.1-8B107
r = −0.221 (p = 0.63). In non-gated Llama-3.1-8B, tokenization fragmentation was negatively as-108
sociated with capability loss ( r = −0.722, p = 0 .067), with no corresponding negative association109
for safety refusal ( r = +0.589, p = 0.164).110

5 Discussion, Implications, and Limitations129
Capability–Safety Dissociation. Llama-3.1-8B NF4 reduced Belebele comprehension by 13.7130
pp in LRLs, whereas the preregistered safety interaction was not signiﬁcant ( β = 0 .027, p = 0 .88).131
Capability loss substantially exceeds refusal changes, indicating divergent effects on comprehension132
and refusal.133
Evaluator Conﬂation: Generation Failure vs. Harmful Compliance. Automated scoring of134
Llama-3.1-8B-NF4 in LRL suggested a −58.9 pp drop, but degenerate outputs distort automated135
scoring. Categorization of all 945 LRL outputs conﬁrms zero actionable harmful assistance (Wild-136
Guard scored 99.7% non-harmful, 942/945; manual adjudication conﬁrmed 0.0% actionable harm,137
0/945). Instead, 69.6% were degenerate repetition loops ( ≥ 500 chars), 31.0% triggered cross-138
evaluator disagreement, and 8.1% were unﬂagged valid completions (overlapping categories).139
Scope of Conclusions. These results are limited to MultiJAIL refusal, tested NF4 setups, two140
model families, and greedy decoding. Among 945 audited LRL outputs, zero actionable harmful141
assistance was found, but this does not establish safety beyond evaluated prompts and criteria.142
Implications and Conclusion. NF4 reduces multilingual capability without producing low-143
resource refusal collapse. Output degeneration distorts automated scoring, creating apparent re-144
fusal loss without harmful assistance (limitations: n=200 validation, MultiJAIL attacks, two145
model families). Non-gated NF4 impairs comprehension without disproportionate safety degra-146
dation in underrepresented languages. Code/data: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/147
multilingual-quant-safety-2026 .148
